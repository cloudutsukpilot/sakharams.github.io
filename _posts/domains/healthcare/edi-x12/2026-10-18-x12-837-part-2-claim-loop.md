Loop 2300 is where the claim stops being an envelope and becomes a medical bill. It holds the claim's ID and total, where the care happened, the key dates, the prior authorisation number, every diagnosis, repricing results, the providers involved, and whatever other insurance has already paid. If you only learn one loop of the 837, learn this one.

This is part 2 of the 837 series. [Part 1](/posts/domains/healthcare/edi-x12/x12-837-part-1-header-loops/) covered the envelopes and hierarchy; [Part 3](/posts/domains/healthcare/edi-x12/x12-837-part-3-service-lines/) covers the service lines.

## CLM: the claim itself

```text
CLM*PCN000123*270***11:B:1*Y*A*Y*Y~
```

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CLM01` | Patient control number: the provider's claim ID, tracked from submission until payment |
| `CLM02` | Total claim charge |
| `CLM05-1` | 837P/D: **place of service** code (`11` office, `21` inpatient hospital, `23` emergency room). 837I: **facility type**, the first two digits of the bill type (`11` hospital inpatient, `13` hospital outpatient) |
| `CLM05-2` | `B` for 837P and 837D; `A` for 837I |
| `CLM05-3` | Claim frequency: `1` original, `7` replacement of a prior claim, `8` void, and, on institutional claims, `5` late charges |
| `CLM06`–`CLM09` | Provider signature, assignment and release-of-information indicators |

When `CLM05-3` is `7` or `8`, the claim being replaced or voided must be identified in `REF*F8` (more on that below). Late-charge claims (`5`) are common for hospitals, which often bill extra charges separately after the original claim.

## DTP: claim dates

Each date segment has a qualifier (`DTP01`), a format (`DTP02`: `D8` for a single date, `RD8` for a range), and the value (`DTP03`).

| Qualifier | Date | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| `431` | Onset of current illness | P |
| `439` | Accident | P, D |
| `454` | Initial treatment, for follow-ups such as physical therapy | P |
| `304` | Last seen | P |
| `435` | Admission | P, I |
| `096` | Discharge | P, I |
| `434` | Statement period (`RD8` range) | I |
| `452` | Orthodontic appliance placement | D |
| `472` | Service date | D |
| `050` | Repricer received date | All |

Statement dates always use `RD8` because they're a from–to range: `DTP*434*RD8*20250612-20250618~`.

## CL1: institutional admission details (837I only)

```text
CL1*1*7*01~
```

- `CL101`: admission type: `1` emergency, `2` urgent, `3` elective, `4` newborn, `5` trauma;
- `CL102`: point of origin (admission source), such as a physician referral or a transfer from another facility; newborns use their own set of codes;
- `CL103`: patient discharge status, such as `01` (discharged home).

## Dental extras: DN1 and DN2 (837D only)

- **DN1** gives orthodontic treatment months: the total and remaining.
- **DN2** gives tooth status: the tooth number (`DN201`), status code such as missing or to be extracted (`DN202`), and the tooth numbering system (`DN206 = JP`, the Universal National Tooth Designation System).

## PWK: attachments

`PWK` says supporting documents exist: a physician's order, discharge summary, lab results, X-rays, periodontal charts or dental models. `PWK01` is the report type, `PWK02` how it's sent (`AA` means available at the provider, `EL` electronically, `FX` by fax), and `PWK06` the attachment control number that links the claim to the documents, which are often sent as a [275](/posts/domains/healthcare/edi-x12/x12-transaction-sets-map/).

## AMT: what the patient already paid

- `AMT*F5`: the amount the patient has already paid (837P and 837D, typically outpatient);
- `AMT*F3`: the patient's estimated responsibility (837I).

## REF: the references that matter

| Qualifier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `G1` | **Prior authorisation number**, from the payer's [278 response](/posts/domains/healthcare/edi-x12/x12-278-prior-authorization/) |
| `F8` | Payer claim control number: the original claim being replaced or voided |
| `D9` | Claim identifier assigned by a transmission intermediary, such as a clearinghouse |
| `9A` | Repriced claim reference number, returned by a repricer |
| `9C` | Adjusted repriced claim reference number |

The repricing references are easiest to understand as a story. A payer sends a claim to its repricer with `REF*D9*1234`. The repricer returns it with `REF*9A*6789`. Later the provider disputes the price, so the payer sends it again: a new `REF*D9*2214`, plus `REF*F8*1234` to point at the original. The repricer returns a new `REF*9A*1111` and puts its previous number in `REF*9C*6789`. Every hop stays traceable.

## K3 and NTE

- **K3** carries fixed-format data that the guide has no segment for, by agreement between trading partners. Use it sparingly.
- **NTE** carries free-text notes. `NTE01` says what the note is about (for example `ADD` for additional information) and `NTE02` holds the text.

## HI: diagnoses and procedures

This is the clinical heart of the claim. Each HI element is a composite of a qualifier and a code:

| Qualifier | Meaning | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| `ABK` | Principal diagnosis (ICD-10-CM) | P, I, D |
| `ABF` | Other diagnosis | P, I, D |
| `ABJ` | Admitting diagnosis | I |
| `APR` | Patient's reason for visit | I (outpatient) |
| `DR` | Diagnosis-related group (DRG) | I |
| `BBR` | Principal procedure (ICD-10-PCS) | I |
| `BBQ` | Other procedure | I |

(You'll see `BK` and `BF` in old examples. Those are ICD-9 qualifiers and aren't valid for current dates of service.)

Here's an inpatient example: a patient admitted with severe cough is diagnosed with pneumonia, has diabetes and hypertension, and is put on a ventilator.

```text
HI*ABK:J189~
HI*ABJ:R059~
HI*ABF:E119*ABF:I10~
HI*DR:208~
HI*BBR:5A1945Z:D8:20250615~
HI*BBQ:3E03329:D8:20250615~
```

The admitting diagnosis is cough (`R05.9`), the principal is pneumonia (`J18.9`), and diabetes (`E11.9`) and hypertension (`I10`) are other diagnoses. The principal procedure is ventilation for 24 to 96 hours (`5A1945Z`), and an anti-infective infusion (`3E03329`) is another procedure. With ventilator support, this stay would group to a DRG such as 208 (respiratory diagnosis with ventilator support up to 96 hours); the DRG is either assigned by the provider or derived by the payer. Note that codes in X12 have no decimal point: `J18.9` becomes `J189`.

Professional and dental claims only use `ABK` and `ABF`.

## HCP: repricing results

Repricers return their result in `HCP`: `HCP01` the pricing method, `HCP02` the allowed amount, `HCP03` the savings, and `HCP13` a reason if the claim couldn't be repriced. [Claim Repricing](/posts/domains/healthcare/revenue-cycle/claim-repricing/) covers it in detail.

## 2310 loops: the providers

| `NM101` | Role | Typical claim |
|---|---|---|
| `DN` / `P3` | Referring provider / primary care provider | P |
| `71` | Attending physician | I |
| `72` | Operating physician | I |
| `82` | Rendering provider: who actually performed the service, if different from the billing provider | P, D, I |
| `77` | Service facility location | All |

People use `NM102 = 1`; facilities use `2`. `NM108 = XX` puts the NPI in `NM109`. Secondary IDs go in REF segments, such as `0B` (state licence number) or `G2` (the payer's own provider number).

## 2320 and 2330: other insurance

When the patient has more than one plan, loop 2320 describes the **other** coverage, and 2330 names the other subscriber (`NM1*IL`) and other payer (`NM1*PR`).

```text
SBR*P*01*GRP555******CI~
CAS*PR*1*100~
AMT*D*170~
OI***Y***Y~
NM1*IL*1*DOE*JANE****MI*X99887766~
NM1*PR*2*OTHER HEALTH PLAN*****PI*OTHER01~
```

That says the other plan is the primary payer (`SBR01 = P`), so this claim is going to the secondary; the patient is the subscriber's spouse (`SBR02 = 01`), the other payer has already paid $170 (`AMT*D`), and $100 was patient responsibility for the deductible (`CAS*PR*1`). The receiving payer uses this to [coordinate benefits](/posts/domains/healthcare/revenue-cycle/coordination-of-benefits/) without overpaying.

**CAS** adjustments use a group code (`CO` contractual obligation, `PR` patient responsibility, `OA` other adjustment, `PI` payer-initiated) plus a claim adjustment reason code (CARC) and an amount.

## Common pitfalls

- **Using `CLM05-1` as place of service on an 837I.** It's the facility type from the bill type.
- **A replacement claim without `REF*F8`.** The payer can't find what you're replacing.
- **Decimal points in codes.** `J18.9` is sent as `J189`.
- **ICD-9 qualifiers** (`BK`, `BF`) on modern claims.
- **Missing `REF*G1`** when the service needed prior auth.

## Conclusion

Loop 2300 holds the claim: its ID and total (`CLM`), setting and frequency (`CLM05`), dates (`DTP`), institutional and dental extras (`CL1`, `DN1`, `DN2`), attachments (`PWK`), references (`REF`), diagnoses and procedures (`HI`), repricing (`HCP`), providers (2310) and other insurance (2320/2330).
